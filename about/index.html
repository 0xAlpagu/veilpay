Confidential payments, built on Arc

VeilPay is a working demonstration of what Arc Network's privacy architecture can enable — real encrypted payments, running on real testnet infrastructure today.

The problem

Every transaction on a public blockchain is visible to anyone who looks. Salaries, invoices, personal transfers — the amounts are permanently on display. For a lot of real-world payments, that's not acceptable: nobody wants their coworkers to see their paycheck, or a counterparty to see exactly how a deal was priced.

What VeilPay does

VeilPay encrypts the payment amount before it ever reaches the blockchain, using AES-256-GCM in the browser. The sender and recipient addresses stay public — so the system stays auditable and transparent about who's transacting — but the amount is only visible to people explicitly authorized to see it.

It's not "hide everything." It's selective: you choose what stays private and who gets to see it.

How it connects to Arc

VeilPay is designed to align with Arc Network's own privacy roadmap, described in Circle's public whitepaper.

Two ways to use it

For businesses: run payroll without publishing what each employee earns. Fund payroll in USDC, allocate encrypted amounts, and let employees decrypt and withdraw on their own terms.

For individuals: send a confidential payment to anyone — the amount stays hidden until they claim it.
